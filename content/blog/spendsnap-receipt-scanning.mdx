Almost everyone who tries to track their spending starts with good intentions and a spreadsheet. Almost everyone stops within a few weeks. The reason is rarely motivation — it's friction.

## Typing is the bottleneck

A grocery receipt has a dozen line items. A spreadsheet wants each one typed in, categorized, and totaled. Do that three times a week and it stops being a habit and starts being a chore. The moment it becomes a chore, it gets skipped — and once it's skipped once, it's easy to skip again.

## Why scanning changes the math

SpendSnap removes the typing entirely. You photograph the receipt, and AI reads the items, prices, and total directly off the image in a few seconds. The habit that used to take five minutes of typing now takes five seconds of pointing a camera.

## What that unlocks

When tracking spending stops being tedious, people actually keep doing it — which means the data becomes useful. A monthly average or a "this month" total only means something if it's built from real, consistent tracking, not a spreadsheet that got abandoned in week two.

That's the whole bet behind SpendSnap: the habit only sticks if the friction is close to zero.
