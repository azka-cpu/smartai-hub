A lot of nutrition apps will give you a score for a product: 62 out of 100, a letter grade, a colored badge. What most of them don't do well is explain *why* — and without the why, the number isn't much more useful than a guess.

## The label is already there — most people just don't read it

Every packaged product legally has to disclose its nutrition facts. The information barcode scanner uses isn't hidden or proprietary — it's sitting right there on the package, in a format that takes real effort to parse and compare against what "healthy" actually means for that category of product.

## What the score is actually doing

Barcode Scanner reads that label directly through OCR, then has AI weigh the pros and cons: things like added sugar, protein content, or artificial ingredients, based on the food's own nutrition facts. The score isn't the point — the pros and cons underneath it are, since they're what let you decide if a particular tradeoff matters to you.

## Why that distinction matters

A score without reasoning asks you to trust a stranger's opinion. A score with pros and cons attached lets you make your own call, faster than reading the label yourself would have taken. That's the difference between a gimmick and a tool you'd actually use every time you're standing in a grocery aisle.
