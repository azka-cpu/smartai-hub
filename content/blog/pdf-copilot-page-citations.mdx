Ask most AI tools a question about a long document and you'll get a fluent, confident answer. What you usually don't get is a way to check whether that answer is actually right without re-reading the whole thing yourself.

## The problem with confident answers

A wrong answer stated confidently is worse than no answer at all, especially when the document is a contract, a report, or anything you're going to act on. If the AI is wrong and you can't easily check, you either trust it blindly or you're back to reading the whole PDF anyway — which defeats the point.

## What citations actually buy you

Every answer PDF Copilot gives points back to the page it came from. That's not a minor feature — it changes what the tool is for. Instead of asking you to trust the output, it gives you a five-second way to verify it.

That matters most in the situations where it matters most: legal documents, financial reports, research papers. Anywhere being wrong is expensive.

## When the PDF doesn't have the answer

The other half of this is knowing what the document *doesn't* say. If you ask something the PDF doesn't cover, PDF Copilot tells you plainly instead of guessing — and offers to search the web instead. No answer beats a wrong answer.
